# Large Language Model as Token Compressor and Decompressor

**Authors:** Wenbing Li, Yiran Wang, Zikai Song, Jielei Zhang, Tianhao Zhao, Junkai Lin, Huipeng Guo, Wei Yang
**Venue:** Preprint, May 2026
**arXiv:** 2603.25340

## Summary

Studies whether an off-the-shelf LLM (via LoRA fine-tuning) can be turned into a discrete, variable-length token compressor and decompressor for long-context processing. Input text is mapped autoregressively into a compact sequence of learned latent codes ("Z-tokens") drawn from an extended vocabulary; a decompressor reconstructs natural language or task outputs from the Z-tokens. Unlike fixed-length soft-embedding approaches (ICAE, AutoCompressor, Gist Tokens), the compression length is **content-adaptive** — determined dynamically by the compressor itself, not imposed as a hyperparameter.

## Method

- **Three components**: LLM Compressor (LoRA-adapted), LLM Decompressor (LoRA-adapted), and an optional separate Inferencer that reasons purely in Z-token space.
- **Compressor**: extends the vocabulary to include Z-tokens plus an `[EOS-Z]` stop token; generates Z autoregressively via Gumbel-Softmax (straight-through estimator for discreteness + differentiable training). Realized compression ratio is regularized toward a target `r` via `L_len = (K/N - 1/r)²`, allowing content-adaptive allocation (denser/less-predictable segments get more Z-tokens) rather than a rigid per-segment quota — derivable as an optimal information-theoretic allocation under a global token budget.
- **Decompressor**: reconstructs from Z-tokens, but its output logits are constrained to the base vocabulary `V_base` — forcing internal abstract codes to translate back into human-readable natural language, which empirically improves training stability and semantic fidelity.
- **Two usage paradigms**: (1) direct decompression — decompressor consumes Z-tokens straight to task output (compressor acts as learned prompt compressor); (2) Z-space inference — a separate Inferencer LLM operates autoregressively purely on Z-tokens (summarization, continuation, QA) before final decompression, decoupling compression from reasoning.
- **Training losses**: reconstruction CE loss `L_tr`, codebook-balance KL loss `L_KL` (prevents codebook collapse), consistency loss `L_com` (stabilizes soft/hard Gumbel-Softmax embeddings), plus `L_len`. Scheduled sampling (gradually shifting from teacher-forced to self-generated Z-tokens during training) mitigates exposure bias, substantially improving free-decoding robustness.
- **Sliding-window compression**: overlapping windows (typically W=1024, stride=256) with a consistency constraint on overlapping regions supports inputs beyond the base LLM's context window.
- **Contextual regularity of Z-tokens**: Z-tokens are context-dependent, abstract semantic units rather than fixed lexical mappings — the same Z-token index yields different decompressed content in different contexts, and semantically similar sentences (paraphrases, syntactic alternations) tend to share overlapping Z-token codes (measured contextual consistency C(z_i) = 0.75 ± 0.11 across a corpus).

## Results (Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B/4B base, LoRA r=128)

- **Wikipedia reconstruction**: at nominal 4×/8× compression, effective ratios reach 5.1×/9.4×; BLEU-4 99.31 / CE-loss 0.04 at 4×, beating ICAE, AutoCompressor, Gist Token.
- **Continuation task**: near-flat PPL degradation as available context shrinks from 1024→128 tokens (+0.44 PPL only).
- **Long-text QA (4× compression)**: outperforms ICAE/AutoCompressor/LLOCO on QuALITY, HotpotQA, NarrativeQA; near-best on QASPER (33.35 vs. 33.46 best baseline).
- **Summarization (CNN/DailyMail)**: comparable/better ROUGE than baselines; direct-decompression inference time drops from 35min→17min (2× speedup) vs. uncompressed.
- **Ablations**: scheduled sampling substantially improves free-decoding BLEU-4 (e.g., 58.24 vs. ICAE's 29.46 at input length 100); Z-token vocabulary size sweet spot ~8k (HotpotQA F1 33.35, marginal gains beyond); larger backbone (Qwen3-4B) improves both BLEU-4 and PPL gap to the uncompressed original.

## Relevance to Wiki

A new context/prompt-compression technique — distinct from [[kv-cache]] compression (which reduces cached K/V tensors) and from architectural KV reduction (MLA/GQA); this compresses the **input sequence itself** into fewer discrete tokens before/during LLM processing. Related to token-reduction methods referenced in passing elsewhere (Token Merging, Token Pruning) but is a full autoencoding LLM-as-compressor approach. Worth a new concept page bridging to [[kv-cache]] and [[quantization]] as a third axis of "make the context cheaper to process."
