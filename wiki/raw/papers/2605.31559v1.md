# Functional Attention: From Pairwise Affinities to Functional Correspondences

Xiao, Gao, Weber, Yang, Cremers (TU Munich / Oxford / UT Austin), ICML 2026.

## Problem

Transformer-based neural operators (for PDE solving, 3D segmentation, regression) treat
continuous fields as discrete tokens and apply standard scaled dot-product attention. This
ignores global functional structure, scales quadratically with the number of samples used to
represent a function, and has no principled way to stay consistent across resolutions/meshes.

## Method: FuncAttn

Reinterprets attention as a **functional correspondence between adaptive bases**, drawing on the
functional-maps framework from geometry processing (Ovsjanikov et al. 2012), where correspondences
between 3D shapes are linear operators acting on functional bases.

- Learns adaptive bases Φ (query side) and Ψ (key/value side) via feed-forward networks rather
  than using fixed hand-picked bases (Fourier, Laplacian eigenbasis).
- Projects Q, K, V into a k-dimensional learned spectral space.
- Computes attention as a **least-squares regression in spectral space** rather than softmax
  affinity: `FuncAttn(Q,K,V) = Φ (Q̃K̃ᵀK̃K̃ᵀ + λI_k)⁻¹ Ṽ`, with a Tikhonov (ridge) term λ for
  stability.
- Proven to be locally Lipschitz continuous (bounded Fréchet differential), giving a theoretical
  stability guarantee standard softmax attention lacks.

## Results

- Few-shot sinusoidal regression: up to 3 orders of magnitude lower MSE than vanilla attention,
  1 order of magnitude below "Intention" baseline; beats vanilla attention trained on 40
  observations using only 5.
- 6 PDE benchmarks (Elasticity, Airfoil, Darcy, Pipe, Navier-Stokes, Plasticity): matches or beats
  FNO-family and Transolver (SOTA physics-aware attention), reported as relative L2 loss.
- Also validated on 3D point-cloud segmentation and OOD generalization across resolutions.

## Relation to Existing Work

Positioned against linear-attention approximations (Linformer, Performer, Nyströmformer) — those
approximate the *standard* attention matrix; FuncAttn instead solves a different (least-squares)
problem in a learned spectral space. Also distinguished from the Galerkin Transformer (linear
attention as Petrov-Galerkin projection) by explicitly learning bases rather than relying on an
implicit basis change, and from Transolver's "slice-and-attend" tokens by using a more general
spectral framework.
