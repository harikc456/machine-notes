# Cold-Token Ablation: Findings

The auto-generated per-layer table lives in
`results_ablation/cold_ablation_summary.csv` (regenerated by
`python -m kv_quant.bench.cold_token_ablation`; per-(passage, layer) raw
records are in `results_ablation/cold_ablation.csv`). This file holds the
hand-written interpretation, which a rerun does not touch.

## Setup recap

For each of 25 WikiText-2 passages (200-token prompts), a baseline greedy
continuation (30 new tokens) is generated with `output_attentions=True`.
Per-layer received-attention scores are used to identify the bottom-10%
("cold") tokens for that layer, those tokens are spliced out of the prompt,
and a batch of `num_layers` (35) pruned variants is run through one
`generate()` call. Each layer's pruned continuation is compared token-by-token
to the baseline continuation (`exact_match`, `first_divergence_idx`).

## Narrative Findings

**Overall exact-match rate is low: 7.8% (68/875 passage x layer pairs).**
Removing just the bottom-10% "coldest" tokens from a 200-token prompt changes
the model's greedy output over 92% of the time. Where it does diverge, it
diverges almost immediately: the mean first-divergence index averages 3.7
tokens into the 30-token continuation, and in the four layers with zero exact
matches (7, 11, 16, 20) the mean divergence lands at 1.48-2.16 -- i.e. often
the very first or second generated token already differs from baseline.
Divergence is not trending toward "no effective difference" (which would show
`mean_first_divergence_idx` near 30) at any layer; the largest value observed
across all 35 layers is 6.96 (layer 4), still under a quarter of the
generation length.

**No meaningful early-vs-late depth trend.** Mean exact-match rate for layers
0-17 is 8.0% vs. 7.5% for layers 18-34 -- essentially flat. Mean
first-divergence index is likewise flat (3.70 early vs. 3.73 late). Individual
layers do vary (exact-match rate ranges from 0.000 at layers 7/11/16/20 up to
0.240 at layer 4, and 0.160 at layers 5, 27, 30), but this variation does not
resolve into a clean "early layers care more about local context" or "late
layers are more robust" story -- it looks more like per-layer noise (n=25
passages per layer) than a systematic depth effect.

**Relationship to the POS-attention correlation findings: divergence
undercuts the causal reading of that correlational result.** The
POS-attention experiment (`findings_pos_attention.md`) found NUM, DET, PART,
and late-layer SPACE tokens enriched among each layer's bottom-10%-attention
set -- i.e. the same "cold" tokens this experiment removes. If those tokens
were genuinely low-value, ablating them should leave greedy output unchanged
most of the time. Instead, removal changes output in >92% of cases, with
divergence typically appearing within the first few generated tokens. This is
exactly the "receives little direct attention but is not causally
dispensable" outcome flagged as a possibility in the earlier writeup: the
attention-based enrichment signal (a token is rarely a *target* of attention
from other positions) does not imply the token's presence is safe to discard.
Numerals, determiners, and particles evidently still shape what comes next,
either through the removed token's own contribution to context or, more
likely, through position/structure effects (see caveats below) -- either way,
"low received attention" and "safe to remove" are empirically distinct
properties in this model.

## Caveats

- **Sample size**: 25 passages x 35 layers = 875 pairs. Per-layer exact-match
  rates are built from only 25 trials each, so single-layer differences (e.g.
  0.000 vs. 0.240) should be read as noisy, not as evidence of a robust
  per-layer ranking.
- **Single model, single `cold_frac`**: only `google/gemma-4-E2B-it` and only
  the bottom 10% of each layer's attention distribution were tested. Whether
  the ~92% divergence rate holds at other cold fractions (5%, 20%) or other
  models is untested.
- **Splice-based removal, not masking, is a confound.** Pruning removes the
  cold tokens from the sequence entirely, which shifts the position ids of
  every token after each removed position and increases the average distance
  between remaining tokens. This experiment therefore measures "what happens
  if this content is not in the prompt at all" rather than "what happens if
  this content is present but the model is prevented from attending to it."
  The high divergence rate could reflect the model being sensitive to the
  *removed content itself*, to the *positional shift* induced by removal, or
  to some mixture of both -- this design cannot separate the two. A
  mask-based ablation (zeroing attention to cold positions while preserving
  position ids and sequence length) would be needed to isolate the "content
  removed" effect from the "positions shifted" effect.
- **Exact-match is a strict, brittle metric.** Greedy decoding means a single
  early token flip (e.g. a synonym or reordering) counts as full divergence
  for the rest of the continuation, even if the pruned continuation remains
  semantically similar to the baseline. The results here speak to
  token-identical reproduction of greedy output, not semantic equivalence.
